AROUND THIS TIME of year—fall bleeding into winter—I always find myself reaching for a shacket AKA an overshirt. It’s basically a hybrid between a shirt and a jacket. It’s heavier and more structured than a typical button-down, but lighter and more flexible than a full-on coat. It’s a layering power move, since it makes even the simplest outfit feel put-together. I love that I can throw one over a hoodie, a T-shirt, or even a chunky knit, and it’s dressy enough for the office. That’s why a lot of Men’s Health editors have it as part of their fall uniforms. A shacket is less stuffy than a blazer, but is more polished than a cardigan.

I used to style shackets a little differently—mostly as an afterthought over a T-shirt, just to stay warm. But now, I think about how the layers interact, the textures, the fit—making sure it feels cozy but still sharp. Over the last couple of years, our fashion team and I have tested more than 30 shackets to find ones with the right weight, the perfect drape, and hold their shape after multiple wears. Here’s some of the best options that have replaced my jacket for a lot of my fall and winter fits.

What to Consider

Fabric

Flannel

Flannel or brushed cotton options deliver soft, transitional warmth that’s perfect for early fall or cool spring days.

Wool

Wool and wool-blend shackets offer more structure and insulation, making them ideal for colder weather or dressier outfits.

Denim

Denim and heavy cotton are your go-to choices if you want something rugged and casual—they’re durable enough for everyday wear and can handle year-round use in moderate climates.

Other options

If you’re after something more technical, look for quilted linings, fleece interiors, or even water-resistant coatings that add function without bulk. And don’t forget to pay attention to finishing details like the lining, buttons, and stitching—those are the small touches that separate a long-lasting shacket from one that falls apart after a season.

Fit

Since a shacket sits between casual and structured, the fit is key. It should allow layering without looking sloppy: you need enough room under the arms and across the chest. Many hit just below the waist or at the hips. If it’s too long it drags; if too short it loses the “jacket” feel. When going oversized, check how it falls on your shoulders—dropped shoulders give a more relaxed vibe but might overwhelm smaller guys.

Pockets

The best shackets for men often come with chest pockets, and some located on the side. If you're someone who likes to keep your essentials close, like your phone or wallet, make sure the size of the pockets can transport those prized possessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Shacket?

A shacket is basically a shirt-jacket hybrid—it’s like a shirt but with the heft and warmth of a jacket. Think of it as your go-to piece for those in-between days when you need something heavier than a shirt but lighter than a coat. It’s usually made from thicker materials like flannel or wool and often has jacket features such as pockets and a slightly more structured fit.

How Are Shackets Supposed to Fit?

[Say the Best Fall](https://incretin-path.escortskhi.com/blog/3037112151.html)

Shackets are meant to fit a little on the relaxed side—kind of like a jacket that you can layer over a sweater or hoodie without feeling too snug. They shouldn’t be too tight or too loose; you want just enough room to move around comfortably but still look put-together. The key is finding that sweet spot where it feels cozy but not bulky, and still has some structure so you don’t look like you’re drowning in it. It’s all about that easygoing, laid-back vibe with a bit of polish.

How to Style Men's Shackets?

Start with a classic look by layering your shacket over a plain white t-shirt and dark jeans for a casual, everyday-friendly vibe. For a more polished appearance, opt for a shacket in a neutral color and wear it with chinos and a button-down shirt, then accessorize with leather sneakers or loafers to complete the ensemble.

Don't forget to adjust your shacket styling based on the season. During the fall, add a lightweight sweater or a crewneck sweatshirt under your shacket for extra warmth. In colder months, use your shacket as an outer layer over a cozy hoodie or chunky knit sweater.

And don’t be afraid to play around with different shacket patterns, such as plaid or checks to make your fits look more sophisticated. Various shacket materials like corduroy, denim, or suede will also give extra texture and depth.
