Shooting pain that radiates from the leg to the buttock is most commonly caused by three conditions: lumbar radiculopathy (nerve compression in the lower spine, often called sciatica), piriformis syndrome (irritation of the sciatic nerve by the piriformis muscle), or sacroiliac joint disorder (dysfunction where the spine meets the pelvis). Each condition has distinct triggers and treatments, so identifying the underlying cause is essential for effective relief.

Explanation

Shooting pain from the leg to the buttock might be caused by several conditions. One common cause is lumbar radiculopathy, which happens when nerves in the lower back are irritated or pinched. This can lead to sharp, shooting pain that travels down the leg.

Another possible cause is piriformis syndrome. This is less common but occurs when the piriformis muscle, located in the buttock, presses on the sciatic nerve. This pressure can cause pain to shoot from the buttock down the leg.
