What Is Piriformis Syndrome?

The piriformis is a small, flat muscle deep in the buttock, running from the sacrum (base of the spine) to the top of the femur (thigh bone). Its job is to help rotate the hip outward and stabilize the pelvis when you walk, run, or balance on one leg.

When the piriformis muscle becomes tight, inflamed, or spasms…

It can compress the sciatic nerve, leading to pain, tingling, or numbness down the back of the leg.

Why Your Hip May Still Be Hurting

Misdiagnosis or Overlapping Conditions• Sciatica from a lumbar herniation• Sacroiliac joint dysfunction• Hip bursitis or arthritis

Chronic Inflammation• Repeated strain keeps the muscle in a cycle of irritation.• Inflammatory chemicals sensitize nerve endings, causing persistent pain.

Muscle Imbalances & Biomechanics• Weak glutes or core forces the piriformis to overwork.• Poor posture and uneven gait increase repetitive stress.

Scar Tissue and Adhesions• Past injuries or injections can leave restrictive scar tissue.• Scar tissue reduces muscle elasticity, prolonging pain.

Nerve Sensitization• Ongoing compression can make the sciatic nerve hyper-reactive.• Even minor movements trigger significant discomfort.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

While piriformis syndrome is rarely life-threatening, certain symptoms require prompt evaluation:

Severe leg weakness or sudden foot drop

Loss of bowel or bladder control

Unrelenting pain that interferes with sleep or daily tasks

Signs of infection after an injection (fever, redness, swelling)

If you experience any of the above, speak to a doctor right away or head to the nearest emergency department.
