Leaking fluid doesn’t always mean labor is starting; sometimes it may be a small rupture of the membranes that doesn’t trigger active labor yet.

Explanation

When fluid leaks during pregnancy, it can be a sign that your protective amniotic sac is leaking, but this does not always mean that labor has begun. Sometimes, a small rupture in the membranes can cause a trickle or slight leak of fluid without the strong contractions or cervical changes needed for labor to start. This can happen in the weeks before labor or be an early sign of labor that still needs to undergo further stages. In other cases, very early fluid leakage might occur without any labor at all, and it is not uncommon to experience some fluid loss without progressing into labor immediately. Other factors, such as minor ruptures or even external pressures, can sometimes cause fluid to leak. It is important to watch for other signs of labor like regular contractions or significant changes in your body. If you experience fluid leakage at any time during pregnancy, it is best to contact your healthcare provider to determine whether it is a sign of labor or if it is something else that needs further evaluation.
