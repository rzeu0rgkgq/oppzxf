What Are GLP-1 Medications?

GLP‑1 receptor agonists are prescription medications that mimic the effects of the hormone glucagon‑like peptide‑1, which is naturally released by the body after eating. This hormone helps regulate blood sugar, slows stomach emptying, and signals your brain that you’re full. (2)

Popular GLP-1 Options for Weight Loss

Over the past several years, GLP-1 receptor agonists have moved from being niche diabetes treatments to some of the most widely discussed weight-loss medications in the world. These medications mimic the effects of the body’s natural GLP-1 hormone, helping regulate blood sugar, curb appetite, and slow digestion — a combination that supports both metabolic health and fat loss. (4)

While they share a similar mechanism of action, each specific medication available has its own unique features, dosing schedule, and evidence base, which can make one a better fit than another depending on your health profile and goals.

Semaglutide (Wegovy®, Ozempic®)

Semaglutide is one of the most extensively researched GLP‑1 receptor agonists and is among the most commonly prescribed for medical weight management. Wegovy® is the version approved specifically for chronic weight management, while Ozempic® is approved for type 2 diabetes but sometimes is prescribed off-label for weight loss. (5)

Semaglutide’s dosage schedule is typically once per week. Clinical trials have shown that patients using semaglutide lose an average of 14.9% of their body weight over 68 weeks. (6) Many patients also report reduced cravings for high-calorie, high-sugar foods, making it easier to maintain a calorie deficit.

Side effects of semaglutide use may include nausea, digestive changes, acid reflux, headaches, hair loss, and fatigue. (7) However, these side effects typically subside over time as the body gets used to the new medication.

Tirzepatide (Mounjaro®, Zepbound™)

Tirzepatide is a dual‑acting medication that targets both GLP‑1 and glucose‑dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptors, which may enhance its effects on appetite regulation and metabolism. Clinical trials have found that tirzepatide can produce greater average weight loss than semaglutide, in some cases by several additional percentage points. (8) Studies have shown that some patients taking tirzepatide achieved a weight loss of nearly 27% over an 84-week period. (9)

Tirzepatide is administered once a week and the dosage is gradually increased to minimize side effects such as nausea, diarrhea, or constipation. (10)

Patients using tirzepatide often describe an earlier feeling of fullness during meals and a more noticeable reduction in snacking between meals. This GLP-1 may be especially well-suited for individuals with a significant amount of weight to lose or those who haven’t achieved their goals with other medications.

Liraglutide works through the same GLP-1 pathway but has a different dosing schedule than other options on the market, requiring a daily rather than weekly injection.

Scientific results have been mixed when comparing liraglutide’s effectiveness on weight loss against semaglutide and tirzepatide. While some studies show that it can be just as effective when treating weight loss and BMI, others show that semaglutide can produce as much as 9.4% more weight loss over 68 weeks. (11) (12)However, liraglutide also has a more established track record than other GLP-1 medications, having been on the market for a longer period of time. This longer clinical history provides a substantial safety profile, and for some patients, the steady daily dosing helps them maintain a consistent routine.

Common side effects of liraglutide are mainly gastrointestinal, and can include nausea, vomiting, constipation, or indigestion. (13) Liraglutide is sometimes preferred for patients who cannot tolerate newer GLP-1s or who have insurance coverage that favors it.

Common Side Effects and Considerations

All GLP-1 medications can cause side effects, most of which are related to digestion and other gastrointestinal issues. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation are among the most common, especially when starting treatment or increasing doses.

In rare cases, more serious risks can occur, such as pancreatitis, acute kidney injury, gallbladder issues. (14) These medications are not recommended for individuals with certain medical histories, such as a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer.These risks are why medical supervision when using GLP-1 medication is critical — it ensures the drug is safe for you before you begin and allows for close monitoring during use.
