How often you should dry-clean certain itemsDry cleaning will make your clothes look crisp, but it's a harsh treatment. Too much of that good thing and your wardrobe is left in tatters. These guidelines will help you balance scents and sensibility.Khakis: Dry-clean them every other time they're worn. This will not wear out quality slacks and will maintain the precise military crease that's required.Wool slacks: Clean every third time you wear them. As with khakis, hang them up properly between wearings.Suits and sport coats: Clean every sixth wearing, if you're the sort of fellow who glows rather than perspires. Every third time if you have that spritz gene.Sweaters: Have them cleaned once a season, or as needed if you're spill-prone.Trust your nose to pick a dry cleanerA strong chemical odor when you enter a dry cleaner's shop is a warning sign. According to Steven Brinkman, author of The Men's Clothing Guide, the EPA's clean-air guidelines mean new equipment should emit less perchloroethylene -- the dry cleaner's magic potion.If a shop reeks, the proprietor may not be updating his equipment regularly. Also, make sure he's using the new Teflon-coated irons, which cut the glare left by older irons.Fight closet odorThink of what's in there: balls of camphor, forgotten sweat socks that glow in the dark, jeans that haven't seen water since Levi's stonewashed them. Sound like fine ingredients in which to marinate your clothes? Two solutions: Shell out $5,000 for the cedar-lined closet, or $5 for a bar of scented soap (go for lavender or pine rather than piña colada) to leave out on the shelf. Now go ahead and breathe deeply.How to maintain clothing's colorHand washing and hanger drying are the best color protectants possible, but what are the odds? (Dry cleaning is next best.) If you insist on visiting the Wash-o-Mat, follow these rules from Diana Kilgour, an image consultant in Vancouver.Wash the fabric in cool water, using gentle detergent (liquid is better than powder). For a fun investigative field trip, go to a lingerie store and ask the staff what they recommend for delicate garments.If you must use a dryer, don't let garments bump around in there too long, and keep loads very small. By the time your jeans are dry, your shirts will be bruised and broken.Take the fluorescent lights out of your closet. They have been suspected of creating spots on white shirts and may fade colored ones.
