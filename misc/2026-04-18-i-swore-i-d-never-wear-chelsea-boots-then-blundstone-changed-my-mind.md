# I Swore I’d Never Wear Chelsea Boots—Then Blundstone Changed My Mind

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

THE CHELSEA BOOT has been around since the 1800s, originally built for British horseback riders. Its slip-on ankle cut, stretchy gussets, and leather upper made it a cinch to pull on before a ride—and that sleek, no-fuss fit is exactly why guys still wear it today. But here's the thing: my personal style leans more rugged than the Chelsea Boot's posh heritage, and I'm more of a lace-up guy through and through. Chukkas? Love them. L.L. Bean Duck Boot? Absolutely. But slip-ons? Hard pass.

Then I saw the Blundstone #585 in rustic brown at Huckberry, my go-to menswear retailer. Something about that worn-in leather just pulled me in. Then Huckberry had just restocked my size (10.5), and it felt like the universe was telling me to give Chelsea boots another chance. I was about to delve into the unknown, a laceless foray into the near-200-year-old style I had actively avoided—until now.

First Impressions

The rustic brown color way is what hooked me first. It screams fall, and pairs perfectly with another Huckberry favorite—the Flint and Tinder Waxed Trucker Jacket. Both have that broken-in vibe, so putting the two together just made sense.

Huckberry shipping time was expectedly quick and came with all the clean tracking updates. The only catch: I didn't realize until later that Blundstone's site has a few more colors including Black, Vintage Brown, and Clay. No complaints here, though; Rustic Brown was the one I wanted anyway.

Up until now, I only knew Blundstone by name. Turns out the brand’s been around since 1870 and is basically synonymous with pull-on boots. A true workhorse, Blundstone launched its own Chelsea boot in 1969—a tougher, more utilitarian take on the posh original. With a beefier outsole and durable leather upper, it became the go-to boot for builders, farmers, and anyone who needed a pair they couldn’t destroy. The packaging kept that heritage going—the box included a warranty card and a small pamphlet about the brand's history. Three words stood out: Durable, Comfortable, Quality. I'd soon find out whether or not the buzzwords applied.

Style

Looks-wise, the boot was exactly what I expected. The rugged, worn-in look came as advertised, and I preferred the rustic brown silhouette to the typical Chelsea dress boot aesthetic. Despite its worn look, the leather was smooth to the touch, and the boots came in pristine condition. The elastic gores have this cool off-shade of brown that gave real fall vibes, and the double stitching added a subtle contrast.

Styling it was easy. I paired the boot the only way I saw fit: flannel, jeans, and my waxed trucker. But what surprised me was how well it worked in our New York City office. I threw it on with khakis and a quarter-zip one day and didn't feel even slightly out of place. After a few wears, I realized this boot is way more versatile than I expected.

Fit

Fit was the big question mark for me. I'd read mixed reviews about sizing, and I have a wide foot, which complicates things. Blundstone says it runs true to size, so I went with my usual 10.5. Good call, as it turns out.

The left boot slipped on easily. The right took a little more work, but pull tabs—one on each side—helped me get it on. I yanked on them pretty aggressively, and they didn't budge or twist. The boots came with a worn-in feel and required little to no breaking in. By the second wear, there was much less resistance. Blundstone throws in an extra set of removable footbeds, which is a nice touch if you want to customize the fit. They feature the brand's proprietary XRD Technology, a removable footbed with extra heel padding that absorbs shock and provides extra cushioning.

Comfort

The boots exceeded my expectations. I wore thicker crew socks, but there was more than enough room in the toes, and the leather lining added comfort and insulation. My feet stayed warm in sub-40-degree weather with standard crew socks. If you are someone who regularly rocks thick thermal socks, you may want to size up at least a half size. The socks I wore, though, were of medium thickness and substantially more robust than traditional dress socks.

Another welcomed discovery: the boot was way lighter than I expected. For such a rugged, durable style, I expected it to be way heavier. After wearing it for more than eight hours at the office, I got a real idea of how lightweight it was.

Final Thoughts on the Blundstone #585

I quickly became an unabashed fan of the Blundstone #585. It combines a hardwearing, rugged look with classic Chelsea styling. The boot featured just enough worn-in chunk to give it character, and function matched the style. It's easy to wear in the office or on a hike, and there's something to be said about a boot that can do both. If you're looking for a boot covers a lot of bases without sacrificing comfort, the Blundstone #585 does its job and more. Not to mention, it's a solid everyday option that won't break the bank.
