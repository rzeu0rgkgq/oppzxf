# Left vs. Right Chest Pain: Critical Differences You Must Know Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/15/2026

Does the cause of pain change depending on whether it's the left or right side of the chest?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Chest pain causes often differ depending on whether the discomfort occurs on the left or right side of the chest. Left-sided chest pain is more commonly linked to heart-related conditions such as angina, heart attack, or pericarditis, while right-sided chest pain is frequently associated with lung issues, muscle strain, gallbladder problems, or acid reflux. Both sides can also share causes like anxiety, rib injuries, or pneumonia. Identifying the exact location, quality, and accompanying symptoms of chest pain is essential for accurate diagnosis and effective treatment.

Common Causes of Left-Sided Chest Pain

Left-sided chest pain is often associated with cardiac issues, but it can also stem from other conditions. Some common causes include:

Cardiac Conditions: Pain on the left side of the chest is frequently linked to heart-related issues, such as angina (chest pain due to reduced blood flow to the heart) or myocardial infarction (heart attack). Symptoms may include pressure, squeezing, or a feeling of fullness.

Gastrointestinal Issues: Conditions like gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) or esophageal spasms can also cause left-sided chest pain, often mistaken for heart-related pain.

Musculoskeletal Problems: Strain or injury to the muscles or ribs on the left side can lead to localized pain, which may worsen with movement or deep breathing.

Common Causes of Right-Sided Chest Pain

Right-sided chest pain can have different underlying causes, which may include:

Lung Conditions: Issues such as pneumonia, pleuritis (inflammation of the lining of the lungs), or pulmonary embolism (a blood clot in the lungs) can cause pain on the right side, often accompanied by respiratory symptoms.

Liver or Gallbladder Issues: Conditions affecting the liver or gallbladder, such as hepatitis or gallstones, can lead to pain in the right upper quadrant of the abdomen that may radiate to the right side of the chest.

Musculoskeletal Problems: Similar to the left side, musculoskeletal issues can also cause pain on the right side, including muscle strains or rib injuries.

Importance of Localization in Diagnosis

The localization of chest pain can provide valuable clues for healthcare providers in diagnosing the underlying cause. However, it is important to note that chest pain can be complex, and symptoms may overlap between different conditions.
