AS NICOTINE INCREASINGLY separates itself from tobacco, the drug has been touted for its potential health benefits—most commonly its impact on cognition, with the podcasting Stanford professor Andrew Huberman once describing nicotine as a “neurochemical attentional spotlight.” Below, experts reveal the truth behind some of the most popular nicotine health claims.

Does It…Improve Your Cognition?

Sorta. Nicotine’s effect on cognition looks like an inverted U-shaped curve, so there’s a limit to how much it can positively impact focus, attention, and memory. For healthy people, it can improve cognition temporarily, but only at a low to moderate dose. If you keep using nicotine and go past the top of the U, your cognition will actually decline. Nicotine has been shown to give a slight boost in attention and memory to people with ADHD, Alzheimer’s, and brain fog linked to long Covid—but these people are considered cognitively impaired.

Does It…Boost Your T?

Maybe. A good chunk of research shows a positive correlation between nicotine (via tobacco use) and testosterone levels, but there’s not enough to indicate direct causation or the extent of the impact. “There’s a suggestion that nicotine slightly boosts testosterone,” says Robert Jackler, MD, a neurologist and professor emeritus at the Stanford University School of Medicine, who’s studied nicotine industry marketing for decades. “I can see some young guy going, I want to build bigger muscles and be more virile and I’m going to use these pouches—that’s just ridiculous. Implying somehow that nicotine will make you a young Arnold Schwarzenegger? It’s false and misleading.”

Does It…Protect You From Alzheimer’s?

No. There’s some evidence that nicotine improves memory and attention in people with Alzheimer’s disease by increasing acetylcholine activity—a brain chemical critical for learning and memory—which is typically disrupted by Alzheimer’s disease. But we don’t have any evidence that it will protect us from getting Alzheimer’s in the first place. In fact, cigarette smokers and vape users have been found to have an increased risk of Alzheimer’s due to the neurotoxic chemicals found in the products.
