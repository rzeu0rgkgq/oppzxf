Explanation

Lupus is an autoimmune disease, which means the body's immune system attacks its own tissues. There are several types of lupus, but the most common one is Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE). SLE can affect many parts of the body, including the skin, joints, and organs.

Other types of lupus include:

Cutaneous Lupus: This type mainly affects the skin, causing rashes and lesions. A common form is Discoid Lupus, which causes round, disc-shaped sores.

Drug-Induced Lupus: Some medications can cause a lupus-like condition, which usually goes away once the medication is stopped.

Neonatal Lupus: This rare condition affects newborns, likely due to the transfer of certain antibodies from the mother.

Systemic Lupus Erythematosus is the type most people refer to when they mention lupus, and it requires careful management to control symptoms and prevent complications.
