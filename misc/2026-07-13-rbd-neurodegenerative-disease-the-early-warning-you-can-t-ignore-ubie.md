What is the connection between REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) and neurodegenerative diseases?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) is a sleep condition strongly linked to a higher risk of developing neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's disease, Lewy body dementia, and multiple system atrophy. Research shows RBD often appears years—sometimes even decades—before these conditions are diagnosed, making it one of the earliest known warning signs.

Because RBD can signal serious underlying health concerns long before other symptoms emerge, early recognition matters. Explanation

REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) is a sleep disorder where people act out their dreams. This happens because the normal paralysis that occurs during REM sleep is absent. Research shows that RBD is connected to neurodegenerative diseases.

Key Points:

Increased Risk: People with RBD have a higher chance of developing diseases like Parkinson's disease, dementia, and multiple system atrophy.

Early Indicator: RBD can appear many years before the symptoms of neurodegenerative diseases are noticed. This makes it an important early sign to watch for.
